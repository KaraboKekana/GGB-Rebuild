Self-exclusion

Self-exclusion is a legislated program where a player may wish to exclude himself/herself from gambling activities. In terms of the legislation, anyone can apply for self-exclusion. An individual that is excluded from gambling activities cannot take part in any gambling activity for a period of 6 months. During this period of exclusion, an individual that is excluded is offered counseling and support through the National Responsible Gambling Program (NRGP).

Request for self-exclusion

A request for exclusion can be obtained from the Gauteng Gambling Board and any of the gambling operators through their security departments

02

Non-compliance with self-exclusion

It is the responsibility or the player who requested to be excluded from participating in gambling to ensure that he or she complies with the terms of the self-exclusion.

In case of non-compliance the punter may be denied access to gambling and in certain circumstances in addition a charge of trespassing.

03

Upliftment of the self-exclusion order

Requests for upliftment of the self-exclusion order will only be considered once the self-exclusion order has been in force for a period of 6 months and the player proves that he or she has attended counselling sessions with one of the NRGP’s Treatment Professionals

04

Self-Exclusion Session

The Self Exclusion sessions are free to the user to register for the NRGP’s sessions. Contact the NRGP on 0800 006 008 or email helpline@sargf.org.za

Warning signs of problem gambling

Problem gambling presents differently from one individual to the next. The indicators below are commonly associated with harmful gambling. The more of them that apply to you or to someone you know, the greater the likelihood of a problem, and the more important it becomes to seek support.

Extended gambling sessions

Spending prolonged periods gambling and losing track of time, often well beyond what was originally intended.

Chasing losses

Increasing the size or frequency of bets in an attempt to recover money that has already been lost.

Borrowing in order to gamble

Borrowing money, or repeatedly using ATMs and cheque-cashing facilities, to fund gambling activity.

Withdrawal from family and friends

Neglecting relationships and social commitments in favour of gambling, with growing isolation as a result.

Changes in behaviour and temperament

Displaying irritability, aggression or secrecy in dealings with others.

Frequent presence at gambling premises

Spending an increasing amount of time at casinos and other gambling venues.

If several of these indicators are familiar, the toll-free helpline on 0800 006 008 is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

Myths and Facts

MYTH

Some slot machines are "hot" and due for a big jackpot.

Every spin is random, decided by a random number generator (RNG). A machine is no more likely to pay out after a losing streak than after a win.

MYTH

If I keep playing, I'll win my money back.

Every bet is independent, and the odds don't change because you have lost. Chasing losses usually means losing more.

MYTH

Gambling is a good way to fix money problems.

Gambling is entertainment, and the odds always favour the house. Never gamble money you need for rent, food, school fees or bills.
